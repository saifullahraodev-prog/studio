## 0. Hook (0:00–0:30)

This boat costs about two thousand dollars.
*[small aluminium boat on a lake, price tag "$2,000" swings in]*

This one costs thirteen billion.
*[aircraft carrier fills the frame, tag "$13,000,000,000" slams down]*

Between them is a whole ladder of ships. And some of the prices don't make sense at all.
*[the price ladder, nine steps, top hidden]*

One ship on this list is less than half the length of a big container ship, but it costs more than two of them together.
*[superyacht silhouette next to two container ships, "?"]*

Let's climb every level of ship, from cheapest to most expensive.

## 1. Level 1 – The small boat (0:30–1:00)

Let's start at the bottom. A fourteen-foot aluminium fishing boat.
*[you in the boat, fishing rod, calm lake]*

A basic new one costs about two thousand dollars, for the hull alone. That's less than many phones and laptops.
*[GFX: "$2,095" price tag]*

Add a small motor and a trailer, and most people pay somewhere between five and sixteen thousand dollars.
*[GFX: price stack: hull + motor + trailer]*

Why so cheap? It's a simple shape, made of thin aluminium, and factories build thousands of them exactly the same way. Buy one used, and it's often thirty to fifty percent cheaper again.
*[factory line of identical small boats]*

## 2. Level 2 – The fast boat (1:00–1:35)

Now, remember the six-hundred-horsepower outboard from our engine video? Mercury's V12 Verado cost about seventy-seven thousand dollars at launch.
*[the V12 outboard with "$77,000"]*

And fast boats hang up to four of them on the back. That's more than three hundred thousand dollars of engines, before you've even bought the boat.
*[four V12s on a boat stern, counter "$308,000"]*

So on the water, "expensive" starts very quickly.

## 3. Level 3 – The tugboat (1:35–2:10)

Next, a working boat: the harbour tug.
*[tug pushing a giant ship's bow]*

Shipyards rarely publish new prices, but here's a real one. Damen, one of the biggest tug builders in the world, listed a used harbour tug, built in 2015, for between four and six million euros.
*[GFX: listing card "ASD Tug 2810 · built 2015 · €4–6 million"]*

That's for a boat about twenty-eight metres long. Second-hand.
*[GFX: tug next to a bus for scale]*

Where does the money go? A tug is mostly engine. Two big diesels, propellers that can spin all the way around, and a hull strong enough to push ships thousands of times its weight. It's a tiny boat built like a bulldozer.
*[tug cut-away: two engines, 360° drives]*

## 4. Level 4 – The giant container ship (2:10–2:50)

Now we jump to the biggest cargo ships on Earth. Ultra-large container ships, almost four hundred metres long, carrying more than twenty thousand containers.
*[container ship at sea, stacks of colour]*

According to the shipping analysts at Clarksons, a new one costs around two hundred and forty million dollars.
*[GFX: counter "$242 million"]*

That sounds huge. But for its size, it's actually one of the cheapest ships per metre on this whole list.
*[GFX: "per metre" meter, needle low]*

That's because a container ship is basically a giant steel box with an engine. Shipyards build them in series, often a whole batch of the same design, one after another. Split the price across twenty-four thousand container slots, and each slot costs about ten thousand dollars.
*[GFX: one container with "$10,000"]*

## 5. Level 5 – The LNG carrier (2:50–3:35)

Here's the strange one. An LNG carrier is a lot shorter than the biggest container ships. But it costs about the same: around two hundred and forty-five million dollars.
*[LNG carrier with big round tanks, tag "$245 million"]*

Why? Because it carries natural gas as a liquid, at minus one hundred and sixty-two degrees Celsius.
*[GFX: thermometer dropping to −162 °C, frost on the tanks]*

The tanks are built like giant thermos flasks, with layers of special insulation. One small leak of that cold liquid can crack normal steel.
*[cut-away of a tank: layers]*

And even with all that insulation, a little of the gas still warms up and boils off during the trip. Many LNG carriers simply burn it as fuel for their own engines.
*[GFX: vapour from tanks into the engine]*

So you're not paying for size. You're paying for extreme cold.

## 6. Level 6 – The superyacht (3:35–4:20)

Remember the ship that costs more than two container ships? This is it.
*[Azzam superyacht, white and sleek]*

Azzam is the longest private motor yacht in the world, at one hundred and eighty metres. It was built in Germany by Lürssen, delivered in 2013, and it can reach thirty-two knots, very fast for a ship that size. Its price was never officially published, but it's widely reported at around six hundred million dollars.
*[GFX: "$600 million (reported)"]*

That's more than two of the biggest container ships, for a boat less than half as long.
*[GFX: Azzam = 2.5 container ships on a scale]*

Per metre, a giant container ship costs about six hundred thousand dollars. Azzam costs more than three million. Five times as much, for every metre.
*[GFX: two bars "$0.6M / m" vs "$3.3M / m"]*

## 7. Level 7 – The cruise ship (4:20–4:55)

Now the biggest cruise ship in the world, Icon of the Seas.
*[Icon of the Seas, waterslides, pools]*

Steel cutting started in June 2021, and it was delivered at the end of 2023, after just over nine hundred days of work. Royal Caribbean never published an official price, but it's reported at around two billion dollars.
*[GFX: counter "$2 billion"]*

For that price, you get a floating city: pools, water slides, restaurants, and room for thousands of guests.
*[cut-away decks filling with tiny people]*

## 8. Level 8 – The nuclear submarine (4:55–5:25)

Then there are ships most people never see. An American Virginia-class attack submarine.
*[submarine surfacing, dark hull]*

According to the U.S. Congressional Research Service, each new one now costs around four and a half to five billion dollars.
*[GFX: "$4.5–5 billion each"]*

That's more than two Icon of the Seas, for a ship that spends its life hidden underwater.

Part of that price is its nuclear reactor. It's designed to run for the whole life of the submarine, more than thirty years, without ever being refuelled.
*[cut-away: reactor glowing, calendar flying past]*

## 9. Final level – The aircraft carrier (5:25–6:05)

And at the top: USS Gerald R. Ford, the most expensive warship ever built.
*[the carrier, jets on deck, top step lights up]*

Its construction cost is about thirteen billion dollars. On top of that, billions more were spent designing the new class.
*[GFX: counter "$13,000,000,000"]*

It's three hundred and thirty-seven metres long, weighs about one hundred thousand tonnes, and carries more than seventy-five aircraft and around four and a half thousand people.
*[GFX: stats card]*

Divide the price by its weight, and every single kilogram of this ship cost about one hundred and thirty dollars.
*[GFX: 1 kg weight with "$130"]*

For the price of this one ship, you could buy more than fifty of the biggest container ships in the world.
*[GFX: 54 container ship icons fill the screen]*

## 10. The end (6:05–6:25)

So from a two-thousand-dollar fishing boat to a thirteen-billion-dollar carrier, ships cost what their job demands: size is cheap, but cold, luxury, and war are expensive.
*[full price ladder lit up]*

And buying a ship is only the start. If you want to know what it costs just to hire one, watch this video next.
*[end screen: "$2.5 Million Cost of Hiring a Cargo Ship" + subscribe]*

## Fact notes (please read before recording)

1. **Prices are 2026 figures** and change with the market. Container ship and LNG carrier prices are Clarksons
   benchmark newbuilding prices (Oct 2026), not one specific ship. The script says "around".
2. **"Less than many phones and laptops"**: $2,095 base price for the hull only (Tracker Grizzly 14). A loose
   comparison; drop it if you prefer.
3. **$308,000 of engines** = 4 × ~$77,000 launch price (my calculation). The script says "more than three hundred thousand".
4. **Tug**: the only hard figure found is a *used* 2015 tug listed by Damen at €4–6M; new prices aren't published, and
   the script says so.
5. **"Almost four hundred metres", "more than twenty thousand containers"**: describes today's ultra-large container
   ships (e.g. 24,000 TEU class, ~400 m). General industry fact, not tied to one ship.
6. **LNG carrier "a lot shorter"**: a 174,000 cbm LNG carrier is roughly 290–300 m long (general industry figure) vs
   ~400 m for the largest container ships.
7. **Azzam's price is not official**: reports range $600–620M; Wikipedia gives ~$605M. The script says "widely reported
   at around six hundred million" and calls it reported, not confirmed.
8. **Per-metre maths (mine)**: $242M ÷ ~400 m ≈ $0.6M/m; $600M ÷ 180 m ≈ $3.3M/m; ratio ≈ 5.5 → "five times".
   "More than two container ships": $600M ÷ $242M ≈ 2.5.
9. **Icon of the Seas**: €1.86B (Wikipedia) ≈ $2B; one outlier report says $1.65B. Script says "around two billion".
10. **Virginia class**: CRS gives ~$4.5B (early 2025) to ~$5.0B (FY2026 budget) per boat at two a year.
    "More than two Icon of the Seas": $4.5B ÷ $2B.
11. **Ford**: construction $13.027B (2018) to ~$13.3B (final procurement, CRS/GAO); plus ~$4.7B R&D for the class.
    "More than fifty container ships": $13B ÷ $242M ≈ 54.
12. **Ending line "size is cheap"**: the opinion/summary of the video, supported by the per-metre comparison.
13. **"Thousands of them exactly the same way" / used 30–50% cheaper**: mass-produced aluminium hulls; the
    30–50% used discount is from the Boatzon 2026 guide.
14. **$10,000 per container slot** = $242M ÷ 24,000 TEU (my calculation; slots are TEU = 20-ft containers).
15. **LNG boil-off burned as fuel**: standard on modern LNG carriers (dual-fuel engines use boil-off gas). General
    industry fact.
16. **Azzam**: built by Lürssen (Germany), delivered 9 Sept 2013, top speed 32 knots (Wikipedia).
17. **Icon of the Seas**: steel cutting June 2021, delivery late Nov 2023, "just over 900 days" (Cruise Hive).
18. **Virginia-class reactor**: S9G core designed to last the life of the boat (~33 years) with no refuelling. Well
    documented (US Navy / CRS); worded as "more than thirty years".
19. **Ford people + $130/kg**: 4,539 crew incl. air wing (Wikipedia); $13B ÷ 100,000,000 kg = $130/kg (my calculation).
