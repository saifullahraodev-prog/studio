# Short: "POV: the first flamethrower attack on the British Army". DRAFT for approval

Our own take on the idea of the reference Short (Q0g-PUwRfTw). Same kind of story (fire in a trench,
panic, then the smart move), told in our style and our words. The reference's exact "fake panic and
machine-gun ambush" anecdote couldn't be found in any reliable source, so this script uses what is
documented: the Hooge attack of 30 July 1915 and how soldiers learned to beat the weapon.
Length ~35 s (~110 words). Action-first: every line has an action to show *(in italics)*.

---

**INSPECTOR:** It's three in the morning, July 1915. You're in a British trench at a place called Hooge,
near Ypres. And the enemy is about to use something you've never seen.

*(Inspector in the trench, lantern, checks his watch; then the camera whips over the parapet)*

**NARRATOR:** A hiss. Then a jet of fire pours over the parapet. Liquid fire.

*(German soldier vaults the trench line, flamethrower roaring; fire fills half the frame)*

Men drop their rifles and run. Within minutes, the enemy takes the trench.

*(you recoil, drop your rifle (it stays on the ground), stumble back down the trench, others pile past)*

But the soldiers who survive notice two things. The fire only reaches about twenty metres. And the
tank runs dry in seconds.

*(you crouch behind sandbags; the flame stops short of you, sputters, dies)*

So next time, they don't run. They back off, let the flame burn out, and aim at the man carrying the tank.

*(you pick up a rifle, rise, aim; the flamethrower man's tank sputters; he turns to run)*

**INSPECTOR:** Decades later, the world agreed on limits for fire weapons.
And that's why, today, international law bans attacking civilians with flamethrowers and other fire
weapons.

### Careful wording
- Range/fuel differ by model and source; the script says "about twenty metres" and "in seconds".
- "Let the flame burn out and aim at the man with the tank" summarises documented counter-tactics
  (rifle fire on operators, the weapon's short range/duration); it's not one named soldier's story.
- Protocol III protects civilians; it does not ban flamethrowers against soldiers. The ending says
  exactly that.
