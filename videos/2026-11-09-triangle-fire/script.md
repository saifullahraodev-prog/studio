# Script: "POV: You work on the ninth floor. Why couldn't you get out?" (Triangle fire, 1911)

GATE 4 test video (Phase 4). Target 3:00. Status: **DRAFT, waiting for owner approval.**
Format: second person, the Inspector opens and closes, ends "…and that's why, today, ___."

**Tone rule for this one:** 146 people died, most of them young women and girls. The comedy
points at the *system* (absurd rules, useless ladders, the $75 settlement, the Inspector's
reactions), **never at the victims**. The "jump" line and the death toll get no gags, no
silly sounds, just a slow camera and quiet music.

Word count about 510 → about 2:45–3:00 at the current voice speed, including pauses.

---

## 1. Hook (Inspector, today, ~15 s)
> That door that swings outward when you push it. The sprinkler over your head. The fire drill
> you groan about every year. All three exist because of one Saturday afternoon in New York,
> in nineteen eleven.

## 2. The job (POV, ~25 s)
> It's March twenty-fifth, nineteen eleven. You're a young immigrant, and you sew blouses called
> shirtwaists on the ninth floor of the Asch Building. The Triangle Waist Company fills the top
> three floors: eight, nine and ten. About five hundred workers. Mostly young women and girls.
> Rows and rows of sewing machines.
>
> It's Saturday, almost quitting time. And there's one house rule: the doors stay locked. The
> bosses say it stops stealing. And unplanned breaks.

## 3. The fire (POV, ~25 s)
> Downstairs, on eight, someone drops a match or a cigarette into a bin of fabric scraps.
> Within five minutes, the whole eighth floor is on fire.
>
> Up on ten, they get a warning. They run for the roof, and students from the university next
> door push ladders across to them. Almost everyone on ten gets out.
>
> On nine, you look up from your machine, and there's fire in the windows.

## 4. The exits (POV, ~45 s; the "options" gag runs on the system, not the people)
> OK. Exits. You've got options.
>
> Option one: the stairs to Washington Place. Locked.
>
> Option two: the other stairs. The fire is already coming up them.
>
> Option three: the elevators. The operators make trip after trip, until they can't anymore.
>
> Option four: the fire escape. It twists… and collapses.
>
> Option five: the fire department! They arrive fast. Big ladders. Big hoses. The ladders stop
> floors below you. The water can't reach either.
>
> *(no gags from here)* Some people make the last choice there is. They jump.
>
> One hundred forty-six people die. Most of them young women. The youngest were fourteen.

## 5. The twist (Inspector, ~25 s)
> Now here's the part that should make you angry. New York's law *already* said factory doors
> must not be locked during working hours. The rule existed. Nobody checked.
>
> The owners were tried for manslaughter. Not guilty. Later they settled the lawsuits:
> seventy-five dollars per life.

## 6. The fix (POV → Inspector, ~35 s)
> But New York didn't let it go. A young woman named Frances Perkins watched the fire from the
> street. She joined a new Factory Investigating Commission. Inspectors walked into factory after
> factory, and the state passed more than twenty new laws.
>
> Doors that open outward, and stay unlocked while you work. Automatic sprinklers in tall
> factories. Fire drills. Fire alarms. And New York City set up a Bureau of Fire Prevention.
>
> Perkins later became the first woman in a U.S. president's cabinet.

## 6b. Rule by rule (Inspector, ~30 s; each rule "fixes" one exit from section 4)
> Look at it rule by rule. Every one of them fixes a way out that failed on the ninth floor.
>
> A locked door? Now it's illegal, and someone actually comes to check.
> A door that opens the wrong way, against a crowd pushing to get out? Now it swings outward.
> Ladders that can't reach? Sprinklers fight the fire from the inside, before it grows.
> Nobody knows where to go? Fire drills. You practise until your feet know the way.
> And the alarm? It rings on every floor, not just one.

## 7. Close (Inspector, today, ~10 s)
> So next time a fire drill ruins your afternoon, remember the ninth floor. And that's why,
> today, exit doors swing outward, stay unlocked, and lead somewhere safe.
