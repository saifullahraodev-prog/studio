## Part 3: Editorial improvements

**Restructured around one spine.** The night landing now runs through the whole video. Every section either launches the jet (§2), catches it (§3–4), or keeps the launch-and-landing machine running: the crew (§5), what happens when it fails (§5b), the hangar (§6), the power (§7), the people (§8), and the faster version, the Ford (§9). Transition lines now say so: "Back to the flying", "the people who keep those flights going", "So what powers the catapults…".

**Shortened / removed**
- §1 cut from a spec list (length, weight, aircraft, people, speed, strike group, air wing, *then* 44 again) to one image per idea. "Up to ninety aircraft" and the repeated "44" counter are gone.
- The repeated speed line ("over fifty-five kilometres an hour, for a ship the size of a skyscraper") was cut. The ending's "fifty kilometres an hour" was cut too.
- The "125 landings" line was cut, along with the unsourced "most expensive warship ever built" and the strike-group headcount.
- The submarine "hiding underneath" was cut. It isn't always true, and it was a distraction.

**Improved**
- The Forrestal part now builds slowly. It opens on a quiet line ("The Navy didn't learn all of this on paper"), gives the precise cause, and then a single human moment (Chief Farrier). The numbers get their own line and a held silence. It closes on what changed and a dignified closing line. The visual cues explicitly say no gags and hold frames.
- The ending payoff is sharper. It recaps the exact sequence the viewer has now learned (wind → meatball → LSO → full power → third wire). It closes on a one-line thesis ("Throw jets into the sky, and catch them again"), not a spec recap.
- The EMALS explanation is now a picture you can animate: spinning wheels charge up, then a flash runs down the track.
- The fuel section now ends on a strong visual (replenishment at sea) instead of an inaccurate claim.

**Pacing**
- First turn still lands by ~0:30 ("Why? To answer that…").
- Each section still ends on a small open loop: "far too short" → launch; "even harder" → landing; bolter → "how do they keep people safe?" → Forrestal → "Back to the flying".
- The Forrestal part is about 50 seconds with built-in pauses. Don't trim those in the edit: the pause-tightening step will be switched off for that section.
