On 16 February 2022, the car carrier Felicity Ace was crossing the Atlantic with 3,965 brand-new cars on board, including Porsches, Bentleys and the very last Lamborghini Aventadors. At 8:30 in the morning, a smoke alarm went off on deck one. Two weeks later, the ship and every car on it were at the bottom of the ocean.

Nobody died. But the cargo was worth hundreds of millions of dollars, and four years later, a court still couldn't say for sure what started the fire. This is the story of that morning, why the ship's foam system failed, why car carrier fires are so hard to put out, and the court case that followed.

📊 KEY FACTS
• Ship: Felicity Ace, car carrier, ~200 m, built 2005, 11 car decks, room for 5,000+ cars
• Cargo: 3,965 vehicles, 574 of them electric (Panama Maritime Authority report)
• About 1,100 Porsches, 189 Bentleys and 15 Lamborghini Aventador Ultimae
• Cargo value: around $400 million (insurance analyst estimate)
• Fire detected 08:30, 16 February 2022, deck 1; the foam system's fans did not start; all 22 crew rescued
• Sank under tow, 1 March 2022, 09:11
• May 2026: a court in Stuttgart rejected the owner's and insurers' claim against Porsche (not convinced a Taycan started the fire; appeal possible)

We remember the crew member who died in the 2023 Fremantle Highway fire.
