The Costa Concordia cost about €450 million to build. Getting her off a rock and into a scrapyard cost more than three times that.

On 13 January 2012, at 21:45, the cruise ship hit rocks off the Italian island of Giglio with 4,229 people on board. Thirty-two people died. This is the story of that night, as the official investigation describes it, and of the huge engineering job that followed: rolling a ship almost 300 metres long back upright, floating her, and towing her to Genoa.

📊 KEY FACTS
• Ship: Costa Concordia, cruise ship, ~290 m, built 2006 in Italy, 114,147 GT, room for 3,780 passengers
• On board: 3,206 passengers + 1,023 crew = 4,229
• Hit the Scole rocks off Giglio at 21:45:07, 13 January 2012, at about 15.5–16 knots; a 53 m gash below the waterline
• Distress call 22:38; abandon ship ordered 22:54; 4,194 people rescued by 06:17, 3 more found alive two days later
• 32 people died. A salvage diver also died during the salvage work
• Parbuckling (rolled upright): 16–17 September 2013, about 19 hours
• Refloated July 2014; towed to Genoa 23–27 July 2014; scrapping finished 2017, about 90% recycled
• Whole operation: about €1.5 billion (~$2 billion), according to Costa's chief executive
• Captain Francesco Schettino: 16 years in prison, upheld by Italy's highest court in May 2017

We remember the 32 people who lost their lives, and the diver who died during the salvage.
