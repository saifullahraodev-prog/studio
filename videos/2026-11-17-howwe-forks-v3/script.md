**Central question:** if eating is the most universal thing humans do, why did we never agree on how to do it?

**Central answer:** it depends on *where the cutting happens*:
- in the kitchen gives you chopsticks;
- at the table gives you a knife and fork;
- in the bread gives you the hand.

Those choices still shape our tables, our manners, and maybe even our teeth.

## Quality control (system checklist)
1. **Is the opening compelling?** Yes. A familiar moment (the plate arrives), then three real contradictions (Shanghai, Indian home, Bangkok), a clear question, and a concrete promise (the answer ends "inside your own mouth"), all inside 40 seconds.
2. **Does the story keep developing?** Yes. Each section answers the previous complication, following the chain in the table above. No section restates another.
3. **Are there genuinely surprising discoveries?**
   - the oldest chopsticks were cooking tools;
   - Japan's funeral rules;
   - silver doesn't detect arsenic;
   - armed dinner guests;
   - "Furcifer";
   - round knife tips from a cardinal and a king;
   - the overbite theory;
   - gursha;
   - why Thai food comes with a spoon.
4. **Are both sides explored?** Yes, all three traditions get their own reasons (fuel, carving, bread). The myth segment explicitly rejects "forks are civilised".
5. **Is every important claim verifiable?** Yes. Key claims have two or more sources. Disputed points are worded as story, legend or theory, and 6 TODOs are marked for stronger sources before publishing.
6. **Can an editor visualise every paragraph?** Yes. Every section has a planned visual in the table above. One new asset is needed: the teeth/bite diagram.
7. **Are there repetitive explanations?** Fixed. The "where does the cutting happen" idea is set up once, used as the spine, and paid off in the ending, not repeated in between.
8. **Does the ending shift perspective?** Yes. It returns to the same plate: each object is traced to a person and a problem, and "one of them may even be shaping your teeth". Then a natural next question (chilies, Korea vs Japan).
