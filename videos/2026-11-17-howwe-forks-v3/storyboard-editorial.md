Revision of the 91-scene v1 (kept as `storyboard-editorial-v1.md`). The visual identity, palette, character bible and locations are unchanged unless a scene note says otherwise. The narration is untouched; recommended narration changes are listed separately in Part B.

## PART A: Overall audit

**What is already strong (preserved)**
- **The structure:** "where does the cutting happen?" turns three answers into one argument. The visuals carry it, with the split screen at scene 13 and its mirror at scene 32.
- **The callbacks:**
  - the diner's plate (1, 80);
  - the mouth teaser (9, 53);
  - the four-tool grid (6, 85);
  - the Delhi family (4, 63);
  - the Bangkok diner (5, 70).
  
  These are the backbone of retention, and all of them stay.
- **The cultural scenes** show real actions in specific households: the Shanghai grandmother serving, the Delhi grandfather's roti scoop, the injera tear-fold-lift, and gursha. These are the channel's identity.
- **The style system:** one style block, one character bible and one location list, merged into every prompt.

**What needed improvement**
1. **Four of my own visual additions were wrong or unsupported.** None of them were in the narration:
   - Coryat striding over the Alps: he was carried up in a chair.
   - A Joseon court lady "testing for poison": not well sourced.
   - Joseon officials in *gat* hats: court dress is the *samo* cap.
   - A lazy Susan in a Shanghai home: mostly a restaurant fixture.
2. **Too much simultaneous motion.** Fourteen scenes asked for several characters to act at once: four synchronised panels, morphing insets, sketches that draw themselves, animated faces in reflections. These are hard to generate consistently, so they're simplified to one action per scene, with highlights done as edit overlays.
3. **Some history repeated itself:** two Rome scenes, a 3-second Venice establishing shot, two separate tooth diagrams, and two scenes for one early-China household. These are merged.
4. **Several images could be reused instead of generated again:** the royal table, the feast hall, the mouth close-up, the opening plate. Reusing them saves generations and strengthens the callbacks.
5. **Balance:** v1 had 30 cultural scenes against 27 historical ones. After merges it's 29 against 25, and every remaining historical scene explains how a tool developed or why it is used.

**What was removed:** no scene was cut outright, because the narration is fixed. Four pairs were merged, and four scenes became reuses.

**What must be preserved:**
- the opening 30 seconds;
- the split-screen idea;
- the noodle–pasta rhyme (42);
- the respectful, motionless funeral image (31);
- the equal-spotlights reveal (79), the one dark scene;
- the return to the plate (80–86).

**Opening (0:00–0:30):** it works and stays as it is. In under 10 seconds, scenes 1–2 show a familiar act. Scenes 3–5 then show three surprising differences in 15 seconds, and scene 6 lands the contrast at 0:26. The central question comes at 0:30 (scene 7), and the "inside your own mouth" tease follows at 0:39. There's no wasted introduction.

**Ending and teaser:** keep the teaser, but tie it to this video. The conclusion lands first, at 8:59 ("one of them may even be shaping your teeth"), so the teaser doesn't weaken it. It also grows out of this episode: Korea and Japan both feature here (scenes 26–31). Scene 87 now brings back the Seoul diner and the Japanese family, so it feels like the next chapter rather than a random topic. If you'd rather end on the conclusion, cut 9:03–9:14. That's an audio trim only.
