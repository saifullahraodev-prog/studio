# Storyboard v2: "Why Half the World Doesn't Use Forks" (creative director audit)

Revision of the 91-scene v1 (kept as `storyboard-editorial-v1.md`). The visual identity, palette, character bible and locations are unchanged unless a scene note says otherwise. The narration is untouched; recommended narration changes are listed separately in Part B.

Scene numbers below are **v2** numbers. Where v1 scenes were merged, the v1 numbers are given in brackets.

## PART A: Overall audit

**What is already strong (preserved)**
- **The structure:** "where does the cutting happen?" turns three answers into one argument. The visuals carry it, with the split screen at scene 13 and its mirror at scene 32.
- **The callbacks:**
  - the diner's plate (1, 80);
  - the mouth teaser (9, 53);
  - the four-tool grid (6, 85);
  - the Delhi family (4, 63);
  - the Bangkok diner (5, 70).
  
  These are the backbone of retention, and all of them stay.
- **The cultural scenes** show real actions in specific households: the Shanghai grandmother serving, the Delhi grandfather's roti scoop, the injera tear-fold-lift, and gursha. These are the channel's identity.
- **The style system:** one style block, one character bible and one location list, merged into every prompt.

**What needed improvement**
1. **Four of my own visual additions were wrong or unsupported.** None of them were in the narration:
   - Coryat striding over the Alps: he was carried up in a chair.
   - A Joseon court lady "testing for poison": not well sourced.
   - Joseon officials in *gat* hats: court dress is the *samo* cap.
   - A lazy Susan in a Shanghai home: mostly a restaurant fixture.
2. **Too much simultaneous motion.** Fourteen scenes asked for several characters to act at once: four synchronised panels, morphing insets, sketches that draw themselves, animated faces in reflections. These are hard to generate consistently, so they're simplified to one action per scene, with highlights done as edit overlays.
3. **Some history repeated itself:** two Rome scenes, a 3-second Venice establishing shot, two separate tooth diagrams, and two scenes for one early-China household. These are merged.
4. **Several images could be reused instead of generated again:** the royal table, the feast hall, the mouth close-up, the opening plate. Reusing them saves generations and strengthens the callbacks.
5. **Balance:** v1 had 30 cultural scenes against 27 historical ones. After merges it's 29 against 25, and every remaining historical scene explains how a tool developed or why it is used.

**What was removed:** no scene was cut outright, because the narration is fixed. Four pairs were merged, and four scenes became reuses.

**What must be preserved:**
- the opening 30 seconds;
- the split-screen idea;
- the noodle–pasta rhyme (42);
- the respectful, motionless funeral image (31);
- the equal-spotlights reveal (79), the one dark scene;
- the return to the plate (80–86).

**Opening (0:00–0:30):** it works and stays as it is. In under 10 seconds, scenes 1–2 show a familiar act. Scenes 3–5 then show three surprising differences in 15 seconds, and scene 6 lands the contrast at 0:26. The central question comes at 0:30 (scene 7), and the "inside your own mouth" tease follows at 0:39. There's no wasted introduction.

**Ending and teaser:** keep the teaser, but tie it to this video. The conclusion lands first, at 8:59 ("one of them may even be shaping your teeth"), so the teaser doesn't weaken it. It also grows out of this episode: Korea and Japan both feature here (scenes 26–31). Scene 87 now brings back the Seoul diner and the Japanese family, so it feels like the next chapter rather than a random topic. If you'd rather end on the conclusion, cut 9:03–9:14. That's an audio trim only.

## PART D: Pacing and animation audit

**Timing:** every scene is now 1.6–11.7 s; none exceeds 12 s. The very short scenes (1, 53, 68, 76, 80: 1–3 s) are stills with a slow push. A 2-second generated motion clip isn't worth it, and stills keep them sharp and consistent.

**Too much simultaneous action, now simplified to one action per scene or an edit overlay:**
- **6:** four synchronised characters became a still grid plus an overlay highlight.
- **3:** lazy Susan, serving and pouring became serving only.
- **38:** cutting, lifting and glances became the fork lift only.
- **57:** morphing insets became three still panels that fade in.
- **60:** self-drawing ancestors became a still plus a push-in.
- **69:** the spreading-dots inset is removed.
- **81:** an animated face in a reflection became a still reflection plus a glint.
- **83:** the blooming sketch became a still plus one cut.
- **85:** the grid collapse became a hold plus an overlay.
- **86:** the guide line is now an edit overlay.
- **49:** two actions became one frown plus a turning wheel.
- **31:** the funeral half is now completely still.

**Merged scenes (same information, fewer images):**
- v1 11+12 → v2 11 (one Roman cutaway with a pan);
- v1 23+24 → v2 22 (one household, two tools);
- v1 39+40 → v2 37 (Venice on the map);
- v1 58+59 → v2 55 (one two-diagram build).

**Reused images (callbacks, no new generation):**
- v2 29 reuses 27 (royal table);
- v2 36 reuses 33 (feast hall);
- v2 53 reuses 9 (mouth);
- v2 80 reuses 1 (plate).

Scenes 27 and 33 are generated wide and high-resolution for the crops.

**Repetition watch:**
- **Plinth or object scenes** are down to 11. Of those, 20, 28, 35, 47 and 51 each explain something an object shows best, and 79 is the deliberate reveal. There are no back-to-back plinths.
- **"Push into…" transitions** are frequent. In the edit, alternate them with match cuts (2→3, 23→24, 64→65), wipes (13, 22) and straight cuts, so no transition type repeats three times in a row.

**Feasibility rule used throughout:** every animation prompt is "one motion idea", either a gesture, a small object move or a slow camera move. Text and highlights go in the edit. This is what current image-to-video tools do reliably.

- **Total scenes:** 87 (v1 had 91). 83 need new images and 4 reuse earlier ones. 29 scenes were revised, each with a "Revision" note.
- **Merged:** v1 11+12, 23+24, 39+40, 58+59. **Removed outright:** none; the narration is fixed.

- **Needing more research before publishing:** this affects captions and sources, not the images.
  - 50: the 1669 edict's text and scope.
  - 33: a second source on carving at the table.
  - 11: a museum source for Roman kitchen forks.
  - 58: the full text of Brace's 1986 commentary.
  - 69: the dating of the spread beyond the Siamese court.
- **Ready for image generation:** all 83 new images.
- **Remaining production risks:**
  1. **Character consistency across 83 generations:** this is the biggest risk. Before any scene, generate a **reference sheet for each character** in the bible (front, three-quarter, profile) and approve them. Then use each sheet as the image reference for every scene that character appears in.
  2. **Hands and utensils:** AI tools often get chopstick grips, fork prong counts (52: exactly 2, 3 and 4) and right versus left hands (4, 62–64, 70) wrong. Check every frame against its prompt before animating.
  3. **Historical costume:** check scenes 11, 27, 33, 38, 43, 49 and 69 against the period notes before animating.
  4. **Clip length:** the audio is fixed, so every clip must match its timecode. Stills with camera moves are safer than generated motion for scenes under 3 s.
  5. **Pipeline:** this is a different production path from the code-drawn "Gallery" look the current video was made with (AI images plus an animation tool). The studio's locked style and golden-frame checks don't cover it yet. Decide whether this restyle is for this video or from video 2 on.
