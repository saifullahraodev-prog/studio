## How it keeps people watching

1. **The hook is story, not numbers** (lesson from Seawise Giant). Lights out, the bridge gone, six men lost, then the twist: "a piece of plastic smaller than your finger". The story starts at 0:32.
2. **Quick-fire montages** where the script lists things: "The steering. The lights. The radios." gets one fast shot per word.
3. **Ship Guy gags only in the technical parts.** He stamps on a brake pedal that doesn't exist, tries to stop the hull with his hands, loses his hat in the second blackout, hunts for the wire with a magnifying glass for a month, gets sunglasses at the CIA crane, and covers his ears at the blast.
4. **Respect where it matters.** From the mayday to the last recovery (3:12–4:38): quiet music, slow, calm shots, no gags, and a moment of silence at "Six road workers died."
5. **Running thread:** the tiny plastic label appears in the hook, at the reveal (7:15), and in his hand in the last shot.
