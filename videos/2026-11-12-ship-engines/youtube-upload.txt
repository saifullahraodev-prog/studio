A piston that weighs 5.5 tonnes. A crankshaft that weighs 300 tonnes. An engine taller than a four-storey building that turns slower than your car at a red light.

We climb every level of ship engine: the outboard on a small boat, the tugboat that pushes giants, the record-breaking four-stroke, the jet engine inside a warship, the cruise ship that is really a floating power station, the nuclear carrier that runs for decades without new fuel, and finally the Wärtsilä-Sulzer RT-flex96C, the biggest single engine ever put inside a ship.

📊 THE RT-FLEX96C IN NUMBERS (14 cylinders)
• Power: 80,080 kW (107,390 hp)
• Height: 13.5 m · Length: 26.6 m
• Weight: over 2,300 tonnes · Crankshaft: 300 tonnes
• Cylinder bore: 96 cm · Stroke: 2.5 m
• Speed: about 100 rpm at full power
• Built for: Emma Mærsk (397 m container ship)
