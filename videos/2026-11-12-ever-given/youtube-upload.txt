In March 2021, one ship turned sideways in the Suez Canal, and for six days nothing could get past it. Hundreds of ships piled up, and about $9.6 billion of goods a day were stuck in the jam. The first machine sent to dig it out? One small excavator.

This is the story of the Ever Given: how a 400-metre container ship got wedged bank to bank, how salvage crews, dredgers, tugs and a supermoon finally pulled it free, why it happened, and why the ship was then held in Egypt for more than three months.

📊 THE EVER GIVEN IN NUMBERS
• Length: about 400 m · Width: about 59 m
• Capacity: 20,124 containers (TEU)
• Built: 2018, Imabari Shipbuilding, Japan
• Grounded: 23 March 2021, 07:41, km 151 of the Suez Canal
• Freed: 29 March 2021, 15:05, after about 30,000 m³ of sand was dredged
• Ships waiting: at least 369, more than 400 by the time the canal reopened
• Held in Egypt: 13 April to 7 July 2021 (settlement amount never made public)
