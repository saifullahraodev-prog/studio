| "today, every passenger ship must carry a lifeboat or life-raft seat for everyone" | The first SOLAS convention (London, 1914) was written in response and required boat space for all. It never entered into force because of WWI, but nations adopted its rules; SOLAS has required a survival-craft seat for every person on international passenger voyages ever since. Today's SOLAS Chapter III: lifeboats on each side for 50% of persons (100% total) plus 25% extra in life rafts. | [NTSB via Professional Mariner](https://professionalmariner.com/?p=10228), [Titanic & the launch of SOLAS (MarineLink)](https://www.marinelink.com/news/agreement-landmark371543), [UK Merchant Shipping (Life-Saving Appliances) Regs 1999](https://legislation.gov.uk/cy/uksi/1999/2721/part/III/made/data.pdf) |

### Notes and careful wording
- "Passenger ship" in line 9 is shorthand: SOLAS covers ships on **international voyages**. It's accurate for the cruise ships and ferries viewers picture; the description will say "under the SOLAS treaty".
- People aboard is given by sources as 2,201–2,240; the script avoids an exact number and says "about half".
- I left out the popular "first boat left with 28 of 65 seats" beat: sources vary from 19 to 29 people. It can go in the long-form video with a range.
