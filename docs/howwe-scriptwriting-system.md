# How We Ended Up: master scriptwriting system (owner brief, 2026-11-17)

Every How We Ended Up script follows this system. The owner's brief is saved verbatim in meaning, condensed in wording.

## Channel promise
"Everything ordinary was once someone's solution to a problem. We find the problem, and why the world solved it differently."

Every video answers two questions:
1. Why do people in one place do something this way?
2. Why do people somewhere else do it differently?

## Philosophy
Write like a fascinating, intelligent friend telling a story you can't wait to hear the end of, never a textbook. Prioritise:
- curiosity over information dumping;
- storytelling over chronology;
- human situations over abstraction;
- specific details over generic statements;
- meaningful comparisons over isolated facts;
- genuine surprises over artificial suspense;
- satisfying answers over endless cliffhangers.

## Storytelling rules
1. **Opening:**
   - start with an instantly recognisable everyday situation;
   - introduce an unexpected contradiction;
   - pose a central question the viewer genuinely wants answered;
   - no greetings, no channel explanations.
2. **Curiosity architecture:** a chain of connected questions. Every section answers something, reveals something unexpected, adds a complication, or changes the viewer's understanding. Never artificially delay answers.
3. **But / Therefore:** something happened, but a problem emerged, therefore people adapted, but that created another consequence. Avoid plain chronology.
4. **Human storytelling:** relatable scenarios, real historical people, decisions and consequences. Make life back then visible.
5. **Visual-first narration:** every paragraph must give the illustrated documentary something to show: people, objects, maps, historical scenes, comparisons, transformations, diagrams.
6. **Pacing:**
   - vary sentence length: short sentences for emphasis, longer ones for explanation;
   - change direction regularly;
   - don't force a pattern interrupt into every paragraph.
7. **Cultural comparisons:** respectful. No culture's solution is superior by default; explain the circumstances that produced each one.
8. **Accuracy:**
   - research every important claim, with at least two reputable independent sources for key claims;
   - prefer academic books, peer-reviewed research, museums and universities;
   - never invent dialogue, statistics, dates or anecdotes;
   - separate documented facts from theories and disputed points;
   - include a source list.
9. **"The Story You Heard":** every video has this segment. Look at popular explanations fairly: what's true, what's exaggerated, and what historians still debate.
10. **Ending:**
    - return to the opening situation;
    - deliver the central answer;
    - show how old decisions still shape modern life;
    - end on a naturally connected question that leads to another video;
    - no generic conclusions, no begging for subscriptions.

## Length
10–16 minutes, about **1,400–2,100 words**, depending on narration speed and topic. Never pad to reach a word count.

## Quality control (answer before delivering; rewrite weak sections)
1. Is the opening compelling?
2. Does the story keep developing?
3. Are there genuinely surprising discoveries?
4. Are both sides of the cultural comparison explored?
5. Is every important claim verifiable?
6. Can an editor visualise every major paragraph?
7. Are there repetitive explanations?
8. Does the ending give a satisfying shift in perspective?

The script must sound natural spoken aloud.

## Deliverables per video (studio convention)
Each goes in `videos/<date>-<slug>/`:
- `script.md`: readable, with sections and the quality-control answers;
- `narration.txt`: spoken words only, for CapCut TTS or recording;
- `sources.md`: every key claim with two or more sources and the wording strength used.

The scene build (`script.json`, word triggers) comes after the owner approves the script.
